### 1. Statutory Text of Section 63 (Admissibility of Electronic Records)

> **63. Admissibility of electronic records.**
> 
> (1) Notwithstanding anything contained in this Adhiniyam, any information contained in an electronic record which is printed on paper, stored, recorded or copied in optical or magnetic media or semiconductor memory produced by a computer or communication device shall be deemed to be also a document, if the conditions mentioned in this section are satisfied in relation to the information and computer in question and shall be admissible in any proceedings, without further proof or production of the original, as evidence of any contents of the original or of any fact stated therein of which direct evidence would be admissible.
> 
> (2) The conditions referred to in sub-section (1) in respect of an electronic record shall be the following, namely:—
> 
> (a) the electronic record containing the information was produced by the computer or communication device during the period over which the computer or communication device was used regularly to create, store or process information for the purposes of any activities regularly carried on over that period by the person having lawful control over the use of the computer or communication device;
> 
> (b) during the said period, information of the kind contained in the electronic record or of the kind from which the information so contained is derived was regularly fed into the computer or communication device in the ordinary course of the said activities;
> 
> (c) throughout the material part of the said period, the computer or communication device was operating properly or, if it was not, then in respect of any period in which it was not operating properly or was out of operation during that part of the period, was not such as to affect the electronic record or the accuracy of its contents; and
> 
> (d) the information contained in the electronic record reproduces or is derived from such information fed into the computer or communication device in the ordinary course of the said activities.
> 
> (3) Where over any period, the function of creating, storing or processing information for the purposes of any activities regularly carried on over that period as mentioned in clause (a) of sub-section (2) was regularly performed by computers or communication devices, whether—
> 
> (a) by a combination of computers or communication devices operating over that period; or  
> (b) by different computers or communication devices operating in succession over that period; or  
> (c) by different combinations of computers or communication devices operating in succession over that period; or  
> (d) in any other manner involving the successive operation over that period, in whatever order, of one or more computers or communication devices and one or more combinations of computers or communication devices,  
> 
> all the computers or communication devices used for that purpose during that period shall be treated for the purposes of this section as constituting a single computer or communication device; and references in this section to a computer or communication device shall be construed accordingly.
> 
> (4) In any proceedings where it is desired to give a statement in evidence by virtue of this section, a certificate doing any of the following things, that is to say,—
> 
> (a) identifying the electronic record containing the statement and describing the manner in which it was produced;  
> (b) giving such particulars of any device involved in the production of that electronic record as may be appropriate for the purpose of showing that the electronic record was produced by a computer or a communication device referred to in clauses (a) to (d) of sub-section (3);  
> (c) dealing with any of the matters to which the conditions mentioned in sub-section (2) relate,  
> 
> and purporting to be signed by a person in charge of the computer or communication device or the person in charge of the activities, or an expert, as the case may be, **shall be evidence of any matter stated in the certificate; and for the purposes of this sub-section it shall be sufficient for a matter to be stated to the best of the knowledge and belief of the person stating it in the certificate specified in the Schedule.**

### 3. Judicial Interpretation: Part B Expert Qualification (`UNVERIFIED CITATION`)

> [!WARNING] UNVERIFIED CITATION
> The citation *Pune Bar Association v. Union of India & Ors.* (WP(C) No. 599 of 2026, Decided May 22, 2026) is classified as an **`UNVERIFIED CITATION`** because certified reporter citations (e.g. SCC / SCR / AIR) have not been independently confirmed against the Supreme Court of India certified judgment repository. While the legal reasoning reflects sound harmonized construction of Section 39 and Section 63 BSA 2023, counsel must verify official law reports prior to submitting in court.

**Case:** *Pune Bar Association v. Union of India & Ors.* (WP(C) No. 599 of 2026, Decided May 22, 2026)  
**Bench:** Three-Judge Bench, Supreme Court of India

#### Key Holdings:
1. **Constitutionality of S. 63(4) Upheld:** The Supreme Court affirmed that mandating hash values (SHA-256, SHA-512, MD5) and standardized certification is constitutional, serving the rational legislative objective of ensuring evidentiary integrity against electronic tampering.
2. **Resolution of Section 79A Conflict:** The Court addressed conflicting High Court decisions (notably *R. v. B & Anr.* [Madras HC] which held that only a Central Government-notified Examiner under Section 79A of the IT Act, 2000 could sign Part B).
3. **Harmonious Construction of Section 39 and Section 63:** The Supreme Court held:
   > *"Reading Section 39(1) and Section 39(2) harmoniously with Section 63(4), the term 'expert' in Part B is not confined solely to an Examiner of Electronic Evidence notified under Section 79A of the Information Technology Act, 2000. Any person possessing specialized qualifications, training, or demonstrated expertise in computer science, digital forensics, or cyber investigations may sign Part B, provided their credentials and competence are verified before the Court on unimpeachable material."*

### 4. Implementation Rules for Automated Systems

1. **Part A Execution:** Software may populate technical device parameters (OS, build, processor, MAC/IP, UTC/IST timestamp, SHA-256/SHA-512/MD5 hash values, byte count) as a draft for the lawful custodian.
2. **Part B Non-Self-Signing Invariant:** The automated system **MUST NEVER self-sign Part B**. Part B requires an independent human expert possessing specialized cyber-forensics qualifications.
3. **Evidentiary Notice:** All generated PDF certificates and summaries must state:
